JazzBloom begins with a small experiment: a piece of music generated with Lyria, prompted by an interest in Miles Davis’s *So What*. That experiment opened a question we do not yet know how to answer.

**Where are the similarities, and where are the differences?** What would we need to learn about jazz to describe them with more care?

The question is our starting point and a goal for the learning ahead. Listen to the reference and the generated piece, and leave an observation—even if all you have is “something here caught my ear.”

:::comparison

## A question to carry through the garden

Ted Gioia’s *How to Listen to Jazz* will guide our reading. Along the way, we can build a vocabulary for things we notice: [[concepts/pulse-and-swing|rhythm]], [[concepts/timbre|the character of a sound]], and [[concepts/form|how a performance unfolds]]. These are places to begin asking, not conclusions about these two recordings.

As we learn, we can return to the same music with a more precise question. Perhaps an impression becomes an observation tied to a particular moment. Perhaps an explanation changes, or a new uncertainty appears.

## Leave a trace of your listening

Play, pause, revisit a moment. Add an observation to this page, attach a time from your local audio, or connect it to another note. Keep what you heard separate from what you think it might mean.

The first entry stays open. The connected pages will grow as we read and listen, and help us work toward an answer over time.
