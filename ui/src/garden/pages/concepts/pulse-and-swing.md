Alongside the first chapter of Ted Gioia’s *How to Listen to Jazz*, start with a simple action: tap a steady beat, then listen to the notes that fall between your taps.

## Try a small sound experiment

These two original teaching studies keep the notes and tempo the same. In the second, the offbeats arrive later. Listen in turn, and notice how that changes the feeling.

:::rhythm-study

This deliberately clear timing contrast is only a starting exercise. It does not describe every jazz rhythm, or the full nuance of a musician’s swing feel. Touch, accents, phrasing, and relationships between the players also matter.

## Take a question back

In the [[recordings/so-what|reference recording]] and your generated piece, which sound helps you follow the pulse? What happens between the beats? If you hear a difference, can you point to a moment rather than describe the whole recording at once?

We will return to [[starting-question|the starting question]] as our listening becomes more specific. [[concepts/timbre|Timbre]] is another way to focus attention; [[concepts/form|form]] asks us to listen over a longer span.
